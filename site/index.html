Mapping the Immune Landscape of Lung Cancer with Xenium Spatial Transcriptomics

Where are immune cells located within a lung tumour, and which findings hold up under critical evaluation?

MD Shakhaowat Hossain · PhD Candidate, Tulane University School of Medicine

An end-to-end, single-cell analysis of the tumour immune microenvironment in a public Xenium tissue section, with every key finding tested against technical artefacts. It is built three ways (Python notebooks, an independent R / Bioconductor replication and a reproducible Snakemake workflow), all sharing one tested Python package, ist_analysis.

Key findings

1

Predominantly CD8-excluded tumour regions

Only 12% of CD8+ T cells were located within 15 µm of a tumour cell, compared with 34% of non-tumour cells overall. Macrophages, dendritic cells and fibroblasts were prominent along the tumour boundary.

At the 400 µm tile scale, 65% of tumour-containing tiles were classified as immune-excluded, 26% as immune deserts and 9% as inflamed. Python and R analyses produced identical tile classifications.

Together, these results suggest a predominantly CD8-excluded spatial pattern in this tissue section, with relatively few inflamed regions.

2

Tertiary lymphoid structure-like aggregates and a CD8+ T-cell gradient

Six aggregates with a B-cell core and a T-cell rim were identified by density-based clustering of B cells, and their organisation was consistent with tertiary lymphoid structures on the matched H&E.

CCR7, a lymphoid-homing receptor, was detected in 36% of CD8+ T cells within these aggregates but in 11% of those in contact with tumour cells. CCL19-CCR7 cell contacts were 2.4-fold more frequent than expected under a within-cell-type permutation null, and the CCR7 gradient persisted after cell re-segmentation.

Together, these results are consistent with organised lymphoid niches in this section, with CD8+ T-cell states differing between the aggregates and the tumour border.

3

Location-dependent macrophage programmes

FCGR1A+ macrophages in stromal domains expressed higher TREM2, CTSK and LPL, whereas those in tumour-adjacent domains expressed higher MARCO, MCEMP1 and CXCL2 (paired pseudobulk DESeq2 between BANKSY and CellCharter domains, with 1 mm tissue blocks as replicates).

Of 60 genes that initially reached significance, 42 were attributable to transcripts from neighbouring cells and were excluded; the remaining differences were confirmed with the re-segmented counts.

Together, these results suggest that the same macrophage population adopts different transcriptional states depending on its location in this section.

4

Robustness to segmentation artefacts

With the vendor segmentation, 19% of T / NK cells contained epithelial transcripts from neighbouring cells. Re-segmentation with Proseg reduced this to 1.5%, and CD8 exclusion, all six aggregates and the CCR7 gradient were reproduced on the re-segmented cells.

Cell-type annotations agreed with the Lung Cancer Atlas (LuCA; 892,000 cells) at the lineage level for 92% of high-confidence cells.

Together, these checks indicate that the main spatial findings are not driven by segmentation errors, although data from a single section cannot address variation between patients.

5

What the matched H&E can and cannot reveal

Embeddings from a pathology foundation model (Phikon-v2), read at each cell's position on the matched H&E, predicted cell lineage with 57% balanced accuracy on spatially held-out tissue blocks (chance, 14%), above a control based on each cell's Xenium neighbours (45%). Epithelium (90%), smooth muscle and alveolar macrophages (71%) were readily recognised; functional states such as regulatory T cells and CXCL9+ macrophages were not.

Morphology predicted regional gene expression (median r = 0.52) much better than single-cell expression (median r = 0.17), and checkpoint transcripts (PD-1, PD-L1, LAG-3) were not predictable.

Together, these results suggest that routine histology captures tissue architecture and major lineages, but not the functional immune states measured by spatial transcriptomics.

One implementation, three ways to run it

All analysis logic lives in ist_analysis, a package for imaging-based spatial transcriptomics (Xenium, MERSCOPE, CosMx). Platform-specific code sits in a single adapter, ist_analysis/io/xenium.py; everything else works on a standard AnnData. The notebooks and the Snakemake scripts call the same functions, and unit tests on synthetic data run on every push.
