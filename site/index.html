Mapping the Immune Landscape of Lung Cancer with Xenium Spatial Transcriptomics

Where are immune cells located within a lung tumour, and which findings hold up under critical evaluation?

MD Shakhaowat Hossain · PhD Candidate, Tulane University School of Medicine

An end-to-end, single-cell analysis of the tumour immune microenvironment in a public Xenium tissue section, with every key finding tested against technical artefacts. It is built three ways (Python notebooks, an independent R / Bioconductor replication and a reproducible Snakemake workflow), all sharing one tested Python package, ist_analysis.

Key findings

1

Predominantly CD8-excluded tumour regions

Only 12% of CD8+ T cells were located within 15 µm of a tumour cell, compared with 34% of non-tumour cells overall. Macrophages, dendritic cells and fibroblasts were prominent along the tumour boundary.

At the 400 µm tile scale, 65% of tumour-containing tiles were classified as immune-excluded, 26% as immune deserts and 9% as inflamed. Python and R analyses produced identical tile classifications.

Together, these results suggest a predominantly CD8-excluded spatial pattern in this tissue section, with relatively few inflamed regions.

2

Six tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS)-like aggregates

B-cell cores with a T-cell rim, confirmed on the matched H&E. CD8 T cells lose the lymphoid-homing receptor CCR7 on the way from TLS to tumour (36% to 11%), and CCL19 to CCR7 cell contacts are enriched 2.4-fold in a spatial permutation test.

3

The same macrophage type changes state with location

FCGR1A+ macrophages switch from a TREM2 / CTSK / LPL programme in stroma to MARCO / MCEMP1 / CXCL2 next to tumour, in paired pseudobulk DESeq2 on spatial domains from BANKSY and CellCharter.

4

The critical look

Segmentation spillover is the dominant artefact in imaging spatial data: 19% of T cells carried tumour transcripts. Re-segmentation with Proseg reduces this to 1.5%, and every finding above was re-tested on the cleaner cells (CD8 exclusion, all six TLS and the CCR7 gradient hold). 42 of 60 'differentially expressed' macrophage genes were neighbouring-cell spillover and are filtered out. Cell types were validated against the Lung Cancer Atlas (892k cells): 92% lineage agreement, against an 87% ceiling for this panel.

5

What the H&E can and cannot see

A pathology foundation model (Phikon-v2) read at each cell's position on the matched H&E predicts lineage at 57% and cell type at 34% balanced accuracy on held-out tissue blocks (about 4x and 9x chance), above a control that knows each cell's Xenium neighbours. Epithelium (90%), smooth muscle and alveolar macrophages (71%) are visible; functional states such as Tregs and CXCL9+ macrophages are not. The H&E predicts regional gene expression well (median r 0.52) but a cell's own expression poorly (0.17), and checkpoint genes (PD-1, PD-L1, LAG-3) not at all.

One implementation, three ways to run it

All analysis logic lives in ist_analysis, a package for imaging-based spatial transcriptomics (Xenium, MERSCOPE, CosMx). Platform-specific code sits in a single adapter, ist_analysis/io/xenium.py; everything else works on a standard AnnData. The notebooks and the Snakemake scripts call the same functions, and unit tests on synthetic data run on every push.
