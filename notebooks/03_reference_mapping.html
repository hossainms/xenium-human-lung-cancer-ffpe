03. Reference mapping: validate the manual cell types against a lung cancer atlas¶

Author: MD Shakhaowat Hossain, PhD Candidate, Tulane University School of Medicine · Project website · Code

Why: the cell types in the core workflow (Step 6) were assigned by hand from cluster marker genes. That is standard practice, but it rests on one analyst's judgement. Here every cell is also labelled by a classifier trained on an independent, published reference, and the two labelings are compared cell type by cell type. Agreement turns "labelled by hand" into "supported by a reference"; disagreement shows where the manual labels, the panel or the segmentation are weak.

Reference: the core Lung Cancer Atlas (LuCA; Salcher et al., Cancer Cell 2022), an expert-annotated single-cell RNA-seq atlas of non-small cell lung cancer and normal lung (892,296 cells, 298 donors), downloaded from CELLxGENE (CC BY 4.0).

Method: the CellTypist recipe (Domínguez Conde et al., Science 2022): logistic regression on log-normalised, scaled expression, trained only on the genes shared by the atlas and the Xenium panel.

Part Content
A Prepare the reference: download, restrict to panel genes, check sequencing depth
B Train the classifier and measure its ceiling on held-out atlas donors
C Annotate both segmentations (10x and Proseg, from notebook 02)
D Agreement with the manual labels: lineage, cell type, segmentation, causes of disagreement
E Export and conclusions

Part A: Prepare the reference¶

A1. Download the atlas and restrict it to the panel genes¶

Purpose: obtain the reference and reduce it to the genes the Xenium panel measures, since a classifier for Xenium cells can only use genes both data sets share.

Approach: download the LuCA core atlas .h5ad from CELLxGENE if requested, then stream it in blocks of 50,000 cells (reference.build_panel_reference), keep raw counts (raw.X) for the panel genes and cache the result. Genes are matched by Ensembl ID, not symbol, so renamed genes are not lost.

Expected output: atlas size (cells, donors, datasets), the number of panel genes found, and the list of absent genes. Check that the absent genes are not markers needed for lung cell types.

Note: the atlas is a 12.9 GB download; DOWNLOAD_ATLAS = False (default) stops with an error if neither the atlas nor the cache exists. Building the panel-gene cache (~80 MB) takes ~17 min and runs only if the cache is missing. anndata's backed mode was far slower than block streaming for this row selection.

A2. Training cells and sequencing depth¶

Purpose: choose atlas cells comparable to the Xenium section and check that both data sets have similar depth on the shared genes. A classifier trained on much deeper cells would expect markers that Xenium rarely detects, and the reference would then need down-sampling first.

Approach: keep atlas cells from lung tissue (no lymph node or metastasis sites), from UMI-based platforms (Smart-seq2 counts reads, not molecules) and with a label (cell_type_major, excluding other), via reference.eligible. Compare the distribution of counts per cell on the 354 shared genes between eligible atlas cells and Xenium cells (10x segmentation).

Expected output: the number of eligible training cells and cell types, and a percentile table of counts per cell. Compare the medians and the spread.

Note: depth on the shared genes is the relevant comparison, not total depth: scRNA-seq spreads its counts over ~18,000 genes, while Xenium spends all of its counts on the panel.

Part B: Train the classifier¶

B1. Training, with held-out donors as a ceiling¶

Purpose: train the reference classifier and measure how well 354 panel genes can separate the 23 atlas types at all, before any Xenium-specific problem (segmentation, platform) enters. This ceiling sets the best agreement a manual type can reach in Part D.

Approach:

  • Donor split: 20% of donors (not cells) are held out (reference.donor_split), because cells from one donor resemble each other and a random-cell split would overstate accuracy.
  • Balanced training set: at most 5,000 cells per type, so abundant types (alveolar macrophages: 114,163 eligible cells) do not drown rare ones (mature DCs: 1,928).
  • Normalisation: each cell scaled to 10,000 counts over the shared genes only, then log1p, identically for atlas and Xenium cells (reference.lognorm).
  • Model: the CellTypist recipe (scale each gene, L2-regularised multinomial logistic regression), fit directly with scikit-learn (reference.train_model). The model is saved as luca_panel_genes_model.pkl.

Expected output: training size and time, overall accuracy on unseen donors, and a bar chart of per-type accuracy (dashed line = overall). Inspect which types fall well below the line.

Note: the celltypist package itself is not called. Its default trainer (v1.7.1) passes an option (multi_class) that scikit-learn 1.9 removed, and its SGD fallback fits one-vs-rest models whose probabilities were not calibrated on Xenium cells. A single multinomial model gives probabilities that sum to 1 across the 23 types. Overwrites the saved model.

Part C: Annotate the Xenium cells¶

Purpose: label every Xenium cell with the reference classifier, in both segmentations, so the manual labels and the effect of segmentation can be tested against the same model.

Approach: apply the model (reference.predict) after the same normalisation as in training to:

  • 10x segmentation: the 154,394 QC-passing cells of the core workflow (Step 6);
  • Proseg: the re-segmented cells from notebook 02, after the same QC as notebook 02 Part E1 (proseg.qc_proseg).

Each cell receives the most probable atlas type and its probability.

Expected output: a summary (cells annotated, median probability, % of cells with probability ≥ 0.5) and the number of cells per atlas type in each segmentation. Compare confidence between segmentations.

Note: probabilities express confidence among the 23 atlas types only; a cell type absent from the atlas (for example lymphatic endothelium) is forced onto the nearest available type.

Part D: Agreement with the manual labels¶

D1. Lineage level¶

Purpose: test the broadest claim first: do the manual lineages match an independent reference?

Approach: map atlas types to the 7 Step 6 lineages (reference.ATLAS_LINEAGE; for example Tumor cells, Alveolar cell type 2 and Ciliated are Epithelial) and cross-tabulate against the manual lineage for high-confidence cells only, since mixed cells are by definition not one cell type.

Expected output: a heatmap of % of each manual lineage (rows) by atlas lineage (columns), and the overall agreement. Read across each row to see where the atlas places those cells.

Note: the overall figure is weighted by abundance (epithelium is about 60% of cells), so per-row agreement matters more than the total.

D2. Cell type by cell type¶

Purpose: test each manual cell type individually, because lineage agreement can hide failures at the cell-type level.

Approach: for every manual type, the atlas types that would confirm it were written down before comparing (reference.CONFIRMED_BY; for example CD163+ macrophage is confirmed by Macrophage, Alveolar macrophage by Macrophage alveolar, the S100A12+ monocyte group by Monocyte or Neutrophils). reference.agreement reports, per type, the % of cells the atlas confirms, the most common atlas call, the median probability, and the atlas ceiling from Part B for the matching type.

Expected output: a table with one row per manual type, coloured by % confirmed. Compare each type's agreement with its ceiling: a manual type cannot be confirmed more often than the atlas recognises its own version of that type.

Note: the assertion stops the cell if any manual type lacks a written expectation, so no type is judged by a rule chosen after seeing the result.

D3. Does cleaner segmentation raise agreement?¶

Purpose: test whether spillover was lowering agreement, by holding everything fixed except the counts.

Approach: pair cells by their 10x id and recompute D2's % confirmed with 10x counts and with Proseg counts, using the same manual labels and the same classifier. A second table follows the Step 6 mixed cells and reports the most common atlas lineage under each segmentation.

Expected output: a per-type table sorted by change in points (negative = lower agreement with Proseg counts), and a table of the mixed groups with their top atlas lineage before and after.

Note: this comparison is biased in favour of 10x. The manual labels were made from the 10x counts, so a cell was called mregDC partly because its 10x counts contained LAMP3; measured any other way, the same cells regress toward the average. The mixed cells, which were not selected for a clean marker, are the fairer test.

D4. Why do some types disagree? Spillover vs platform¶

Purpose: separate two causes of poor agreement that need different responses:

  • Spillover (segmentation): Xenium cells contain transcripts of a neighbouring lineage that the atlas version of the type does not express;
  • Platform difference (chemistry): a defining marker is detected far less often by Xenium probes than by scRNA-seq, so the cell looks like a different type to a model trained on scRNA-seq.

Approach: for four poorly confirmed types, compare the % of cells with at least one count of three own markers and three foreign markers, in high-confidence Xenium cells and in the matching eligible atlas cells, and report the ratio.

Expected output: a table per type and gene with Xenium %, atlas % and the Xenium / atlas ratio. Own markers well below 1x point to a platform gap; foreign markers well above 1x point to spillover.

Note: the atlas has no lymphatic endothelial type, so lymphatic cells are compared with all atlas endothelial cells. The foreign markers tested are T-cell genes (or CD4-lineage genes for CD8 T); other contaminants are not tested here.

Conclusions¶

  1. Lineages are largely supported. 92% of high-confidence cells receive the same lineage from an independent reference as from the manual annotation. That figure is carried by epithelium (99%), myeloid (91%) and stroma (90%); T / NK cells agree least (63%), with 15% called B / plasma and 15% myeloid. The ceiling for this panel on held-out atlas donors is 87% at the 23-type level.
  2. Confirmed at cell-type level: smooth muscle, fibroblast, ciliated, proliferating tumour, alveolar macrophage, plasma, B and mregDC cells, and the S100A12+ group as neutrophil / monocyte.
  3. Not confirmable from this panel: malignancy. The atlas places most tumour-labelled cells in an AT2 / club-like epithelial state. The tumour call remains an inference from expression programmes and spatial context, as noted in Step 6, and needs copy-number evidence to confirm.
  4. Poor agreement has identifiable causes: spillover (lymphatic endothelial, mast) and a detection gap for NKG7 combined with spillover (CD8 T, NK). These are properties of the data and do not by themselves show that the labels are wrong.
  5. Segmentation: on cells not selected for a clean marker (Step 6 mixed), Proseg counts let the reference recognise the T cell inside the outline, independent support for notebook 02's conclusion that Proseg removes spillover.

Limits: one reference and one classifier at the cell_type_major level; manual labels were made on 10x counts, which biases the segmentation comparison toward 10x; agreement is measured on high-confidence cells only.

Possible next steps: scANVI / scArches mapping (needs a GPU; better suited to the 51-type cell_type_tumor level), and copy-number inference on the Xenium cells to test the tumour call directly.
